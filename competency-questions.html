This page sets out the requirements of IMPACTO as an Ontology Requirements Specification Document (ORSD) and the 14 competency questions that IMPACTO must answer about the Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) under the GDPR and the Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (FRIA) under the EU AI Act. It evaluates IMPACTO against those questions using three worked deployments, a SPARQL query for each question, and SHACL shapes that check both the ontology and assessment records built with it.

This document accompanies IMPACTO version 1.0. All the files referred to here are in the IMPACTO GitHub repository.

Ontology Requirements Specification

The requirements of IMPACTO are specified as an Ontology Requirements Specification Document (ORSD).

PurposeThe purpose of IMPACTO is to model a combined (FRIA + DPIA) impact assessment as an information process and its information requirements.
ScopeThe DPIA as per GDPR Art. 35, the Data Protection Authority blacklists (DPIA Required lists) and whitelists (DPIA Not Required lists), and the FRIA as defined in the AI Act Art. 27 and Annex III.
Implementation languageW3C RDFS and SKOS. IMPACTO is published as RDF/XML and JSON-LD.
Intended end-usersOrganisations who create and use DPIAs and FRIAs.
Intended uses Use 1: to document processes of conducting impact assessments.
Use 2: to document obligations regarding impact assessments.
Non-functional requirements NFR1 Interoperability: the ontology should extend existing legal compliance ontologies such as the Data Privacy Vocabulary (DPV).
NFR2 Scalability: the ontology should be adaptable and extensible.
NFR3 Usability: the ontology should be usable by legal and non-legal stakeholders.
Functional requirementsThe competency questions in , grouped as CQG1 (DPIA) and CQG2 (FRIA).

Overlap between DPIA and FRIA questions

Six DPIA questions have a FRIA counterpart. IMPACTO answers each pair with the same modelling pattern, which is what lets one combined impact assessment hold both a DPIA and a FRIA for the same process (EX2 below).

DPIAFRIAShared topicShared pattern and difference
CQ1CQ8Process that requires the assessmentBoth are typed processes linked to the impact assessment with dpv:hasProcess; a necessity assessment records the outcome with dpv:hasStatus. One process can be both (EX2).
CQ3CQ9Process that does not require the assessmentBoth use a "not required" process class and a necessity status. GDPR exemptions come from DPA lists (Art. 35(5)); AI Act exemptions come from Art. 27(1) itself (Annex III point 2).
CQ4CQ10JurisdictionBoth use dpv:hasJurisdiction. A DPIA requirement differs by Member State list; the FRIA requirement is the same across the EU.
CQ5CQ11Procedure and its contentBoth procedures share the same pattern: dpv:hasAssessment chains necessity → procedure → outcome with dpv:isBefore, and their content is given with dpv:hasContext, dpv:hasRisk/dpv:hasImpact and measures. Human oversight (dpv:HumanInvolvementForOversight) is reused in both. Art. 27(4) lets the FRIA complement the DPIA (impacto:eu-aiact-ExistingDPIAInput).
CQ6CQ12Changes and updatesSame two categories (predetermined and non-predetermined change) with a review or update status. IMPACTO mirrors eu-aiact:ChangeCategory for the GDPR with impacto:eu-gdpr-RiskChangeCategory.
CQ7CQ13Authority involved after the assessmentBoth name the authority with dpv:hasAuthority and a status. The DPIA leads to prior consultation of the DPA only where high risk remains; the FRIA result is always notified to the market surveillance authority.

CQ2 (roles) and CQ14 (documentation and tools) have no direct counterpart, but they meet in practice: the controller of the DPIA is often also the deployer who carries out the FRIA, and the DPIA can be documentation used for the FRIA (AI Act Art. 27(4)).

Use Cases

Three examples show how IMPACTO records whether an impact assessment is required. They are written in example.ttl; the organisations are fictional. Each example is one impact assessment (dpv:ImpactAssessment) that links to the process with dpv:hasProcess and to the DPIA and FRIA necessity assessments with dpv:hasAssessment. A necessity assessment records the outcome (dpv:hasStatus), the condition that justifies it (dpv:hasJustification), who is responsible and the jurisdiction.

The processes are described with concepts from HAPT and DPV, and the justifications point to the DPIA Required conditions (for example eu-gdpr:C1, ie:C11) and the HAPT Annex III conditions (annex3:C1a, annex3:C2). HAPT is documented separately; the examples only refer to its terms.

EX1A public hospital in Ireland processes patients' electronic health records at large scale.No AI systemRequired (eu-gdpr:C1, GDPR Art. 35(3)(b))Not applicable
EX2A public transport authority in Ireland identifies people in recorded CCTV footage with a remote biometric identification system.Annex III 1(a)Required (ie:C11, eu-gdpr:C5)Required (annex3:C1a)
EX3A water utility in Austria uses an AI safety component to control pressure in the water network. No personal data is processed.Annex III 2(a)Not applicableNot required: exempt (annex3:C2)

EX1: processing of special category data requires a DPIA

Large-scale processing of special category data requires a DPIA under GDPR Art. 35(3)(b), so the requirement applies across the EU (CQ4). The hospital, as controller, is responsible for the DPIA and has a data protection officer (CQ2).

    dpv:hasDataController ex:Hospital ;
    dpv:hasJurisdiction loc:IE .

EX2: a biometric AI system requires a FRIA and a DPIA

A remote biometric identification system is high-risk under AI Act Annex III point 1(a). Because the deployer is a body governed by public law, Art. 27(1) requires a FRIA (CQ8). Because the system processes biometric data, the Irish DPIA Required list (ie:C11) and GDPR Art. 35(3)(c) require a DPIA as well (CQ1). One impact assessment holds both necessity assessments.

EX3: a critical infrastructure AI system is exempt from the FRIA

An AI safety component in water supply is high-risk under Annex III point 2(a). Art. 27(1) excludes high-risk AI systems intended to be used in the area listed in Annex III point 2, so the FRIA is not required and the status is FRIA Exempt (CQ9). The requirement is still decided under EU law (CQ10). The system processes no personal data, so no DPIA applies.

ex:EX3-Processing a impacto:eu-aiact-FRIANotRequiredProcess ;
    rdfs:label "Controlling water network pressure with an AI safety component"@en ;
    dpv:hasPurpose hapt:eu-aiact-WaterSupplyManagement ;
    dpv:isImplementedUsingTechnology ex:WaterSafetyAI ;
    dpv:hasJurisdiction loc:AT ;
    rdfs:comment "Uses sensor readings only. No personal data is processed, so the GDPR and the DPIA do not apply."@en .

ex:EX3-FRIANecessity a impacto:eu-aiact-FRIANecessityAssessment ;
    rdfs:label "Is a FRIA required for EX3?"@en ;
    dpv:hasStatus impacto:eu-aiact-FRIAExempt ;
    dpv:hasContext impacto:eu-aiact-HighRiskAISystem, impacto:eu-aiact-Exempt ;
    dpv:hasJustification annex3:C2 ;
    dpv:hasResponsibleEntity ex:WaterUtility ;
    dpv:hasJurisdiction loc:EU, loc:AT ;
    rdfs:comment "Art. 27(1) excludes high-risk AI systems intended to be used in the area listed in Annex III point 2."@en .

Evaluation

IMPACTO is validated against its competency questions in two ways.

  1. SPARQL. Each competency question is a SPARQL query. Most questions ask how something is represented, so the query lists the concepts and relations that answer it and checks that each one is defined in IMPACTO or in the DPV modules it reuses, with its place in the hierarchy. A missing term is reported as NOT FOUND, so the question fails. The query also shows which examples use each term. CQ4 and CQ10 ask about a particular process, so they can only be answered from instance data: they run over the examples.
  2. SHACL. Shapes check that every IMPACTO term is documented and placed under a DPV parent (NFR1, NFR3), and that a record of a DPIA or FRIA requirement holds everything the questions need: the status, the justification, the jurisdiction and the responsible role. The examples must conform, and a file of broken records must be rejected.

The examples are therefore needed only for the questions about a specific process, and to show that the ontology can be used to write records that pass the shapes.

SPARQL queries

Each query file in sparql/ starts with the question, the law, the data it runs on and the expected answer. The queries need the DPV 2.3 modules dpv, eu-gdpr, eu-aiact and tech to find the reused terms. CQ4 also needs the DPIA Required conditions: a jurisdiction is returned when the process meets every criterion of at least one condition on that jurisdiction's list, and conditions taken directly from the GDPR apply in the whole EU.

CQ1: How is a process that requires a DPIA represented?

Law: GDPR Art. 35(1), 35(3), 35(4). Query: sparql/cq01.rq. Data: impacto.rdf + example.ttl + DPV 2.3 (dpv, eu-gdpr, eu-aiact, tech).
Expected: Every term is found. EX1 and EX2 are DPIA required processes, each with a necessity assessment with status DPIA Required and its justification.
Result: Answered: all 7 terms found in IMPACTO or DPV.

Running the evaluation

With Apache Jena for SPARQL and pySHACL for SHACL:

# answer a competency question (dpv.ttl, eu-gdpr.ttl, eu-aiact.ttl and tech.ttl are the DPV 2.3 modules from https://w3id.org/dpv)
arq --data impacto.rdf --data example.ttl --data dpv.ttl --data eu-gdpr.ttl --data eu-aiact.ttl --data tech.ttl --query sparql/cq01.rq
arq --data impacto.rdf --data example.ttl --data dpv.ttl --data eu-gdpr.ttl --data eu-aiact.ttl --data tech.ttl --data DPIARequiredConditions.ttl --query sparql/cq04.rq

# validate the ontology, the examples, and the negative test
pyshacl -s impacto-shapes.ttl -a impacto.rdf
pyshacl -s impacto-shapes.ttl -a impacto.rdf example.ttl
pyshacl -s impacto-shapes.ttl -a impacto.rdf tests/example-invalid.ttl
